# Design System: knobs

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Setup Path, Set Straight"**

The knobs site is the open-source download page done the way the best projects do it: white or graphite ground, cool greys taken from the knob, black ink for the one thing to press, and nothing on top. There is no concept and no theme, and the one decoration, a voice traced along the foot of the first screen, never competes with the file. The finish bar is the download pages of Zed and Audacity: exact spacing, hairline structure, honest states, and every link visibly going where it says.

The page reads as a path. Setup is four numbered steps joined by a hairline rail, and the download is step 3, held in the one bordered panel on the page. Everything else is open typography on hairline rules: ruled tables, ruled fact grids, file rows with their checksums folded under them. Density is moderate and calm; sections breathe on large vertical padding, and inside a section the type does the work.

Motion settles and never performs. Feedback answers in 160ms, structure settles in 320ms, both on one curve, with opacity and a 4px rise only. Nothing enters on scroll except the setup rail inking as the reader passes each step.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Restrained palette: cool graphite neutrals, ink as the only action colour, brand red only inside the logo.
- IBM Plex Sans for every word on the page, from the pitch to the footnotes; IBM Plex Mono for anything copyable; Sora only inside the outlined logo.
- 1px hairlines carry structure; one tight lift is the only shadow.
- A step rail with round numbered nodes, and a single "station" panel for the download.
- Light and dark follow the visitor's system setting, with a token pair for every role.
- One decoration: a static 1px trace of a spoken phrase along the seam under the first screen.
- Self-contained: the fonts, logo and icons are served from the site; the only outside request is the GitHub releases API.

## Colors

A cool, near-monochrome palette drawn from the knob's graphite, with black ink as the single voice of action. Every role has a light value and a `-dark` counterpart, switched by `prefers-color-scheme`.

### Primary
- **Brand Ink** (`ink`, `ink-dark`): body text, headings, and the fill of every primary action: the primary button, the download button, inked step nodes, the drawn rail segment, focus rings and text selection. In dark mode ink inverts to the light logo ink and primary buttons become light with dark text (`on-ink-dark`).
- **Ink Lifted** (`ink-hover`, `ink-hover-dark`): the hover fill of ink buttons; one step lighter on light ground, pure white on dark.
- **On Ink** (`on-ink`, `on-ink-dark`): text and icons on an ink fill.

### Neutral
- **Paper** (`paper`, `paper-dark`): the page ground for the content sections.
- **Paper Subtle** (`paper-subtle`, `paper-subtle-dark`): the header, the first screen with the path, the footer, hover fills on rows and summaries, and the quiet "Needs" and tip notes.
- **Surface** (`surface`, `surface-dark`): bordered containers: the station, file lists, disclosures, signal-path stages, secondary buttons, copy buttons.
- **Surface Sunken** (`surface-sunken`, `surface-sunken-dark`): code blocks, the open SHA-256 panel, and the nav-link hover.
- **Hairline** (`hairline`, `hairline-dark`): row dividers, section rules, table body rules.
- **Hairline Strong** (`hairline-strong`, `hairline-strong-dark`): container borders, the step rail, idle node rings, table header rules, link underlines at rest, and the voice trace.
- **Graphite 2** (`graphite-2`, `graphite-2-dark`): secondary prose: the lede, step descriptions, section intros, nav links.
- **Graphite 3** (`graphite-3`, `graphite-3-dark`): tertiary text: hints, footnotes, file metadata, table headers, separators, the chevron.

### Status
- **Signal Green** (`signal-ok`, `signal-ok-dark`): only the "Copied" confirmation on copy buttons.
- **Signal Amber** (`signal-warn`, `signal-warn-dark`): only the preview strip's icon and the "Preview" tag, which mark made-up release data.

### Brand
- **Logo Red** (`brand-red` on light, `brand-red-dark` on dark): lives inside the logo files only, on the knob's pointer and "obs" in the wordmark. The stylesheet does not use it.

### Named Rules
**The One Ink Rule.** Ink is the only action colour. A primary action is an ink fill; a secondary action is a surface fill with a strong hairline. No other hue ever marks something to press.

**The Red Stays in the Logo Rule.** Brand red appears in the lockup and app icon and nowhere else: not as a link colour, a button, a marker, a badge or a background.

**The Logo Is Not Ours to Recolour Rule.** Use the lockup files as supplied (dark text on light, light text on dark, swapped by colour scheme). Never recolour, rotate or stretch the knob, never show it under 32px tall, and keep clear space at least the length of the pointer around it.

## Typography

**Display Font:** IBM Plex Sans at 600, self-hosted
**Body Font:** IBM Plex Sans at 400 and 500, the same family
**Label/Mono Font:** IBM Plex Mono at 400 and 500, self-hosted, falling back to the system monospace (Cascadia Mono on Windows)

**Character:** Plex Sans is plain and engineered, with the slightly squared, open forms of a well-made technical manual, which suits a page whose job is to be trusted. One family sets every role, so the hierarchy comes from size, weight and tone, never from a change of voice. Plex Mono is its companion on the same vertical metrics, so a version or a checksum sits on the line beside the prose without a jump. Sora stays in the wordmark alone, which keeps the lockup reading as the logo rather than as one more heading.

**Files.** The Latin subsets exactly as the official IBM Plex release ships them (`@ibm/plex-sans` 1.1.0, `@ibm/plex-mono` 2.5.0, github.com/IBM/plex), five woff2 files in `site/fonts/` with the release's own unicode ranges and its SIL Open Font License beside them (`OFL-IBM-Plex.txt`). The page preloads only the three Plex Sans weights the first screen uses; Plex Mono loads when a mono line renders. Every face uses `font-display: swap`, and until Plex arrives the text sets in "IBM Plex Sans Fallback": Arial scaled to Plex's width and line metrics on this page's own text (400: size 100.14%; 600: Arial Bold at 96.6%), so the swap barely moves a line.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (600, fluid 2rem to 4rem, line height 1.06, tracking -0.025em): the single h1 pitch on the first screen, across two lines.
- **Headline** (600, fluid 1.75rem to 2.375rem, tracking -0.018em): section titles, set in the left head column.
- **Title Large** (600, 1.5rem, tracking -0.014em): the full setup steps' titles beside their sticky nodes. **Title Large Phone** (600, 1.25rem) takes over at 639px and below, and also sets the full download notice's head.
- **Title** (600, 1.1875rem, line height 1.3, tracking -0.008em): path step titles, fact terms, measurement titles.
- **Title Small** (600, 1.125rem, tracking -0.006em): the release state head inside the station and sub-headings.
- **Figure** (600, 2rem, tracking -0.02em, tabular): measured figures; delay numbers use the same face at 1.75rem.
- **Version** (Plex Mono 500, 2rem, line height 1.1, tracking -0.01em): the release version at the head of the full download section. **Version Phone** (1.625rem) takes over at 719px and below.
- **Numeral** (600, 0.875rem, tabular): step node numbers; 1rem in the larger 40px setup nodes.
- **Lede** (400, 1.0625rem, line height 1.55, Graphite 2): the pitch description and section intros, capped at the measure.
- **Body** (400, 1rem, line height 1.55; 1.6 in step bodies, which are capped at the measure).
- **Body Small** (0.9375rem, line height 1.5): the workhorse for step text, tables, notes, disclosures and links rows.
- **UI Small** (0.875rem): nav, link rows, file metadata.
- **Label** (0.8125rem, line height 1.45, Graphite 3): hints, destinations, footnotes, table headers, hashes. Sentence case, no tracking.
- **Mono** (Plex Mono, 0.875em of its context, ligatures off): versions, file names, file sizes in the file rows, hashes, commands, hosts, the github.com destination.

### Named Rules
**The Wordmark Face Rule.** Sora is the wordmark's face and lives only in the logo files, as outlined paths. The page never loads it and never sets text in it. Every heading, numeral, figure, line of prose, button and label is IBM Plex Sans; everything copyable is IBM Plex Mono.

**The Copyable Is Mono Rule.** If a visitor could compare it, paste it or type it (a version, a file name, a file size, a checksum, a command, a host), it is set in Plex Mono with ligatures off. Numbers that line up use tabular figures: Plex Sans's default figures are already tabular (every digit is 0.6em wide), and `tabular-nums` stays declared for the fallback faces. A SHA-256 wraps in even rows of 32 characters (16 on phones), so it can be compared in chunks.

**The Measure Rule.** Every text cap is the `--measure` token, 52ch: step bodies, section intros, the stacked lede, fact and measurement text, the one-column limits list and the station's state text. In Plex Sans 1ch is the width of "0" (0.6em) while an average character is about 0.45em, so 52ch sets lines of about 66 to 72 characters at any text size. Never cap text in rem, and never read "68ch" as 68 characters.

**The No Small Caps Rule.** Labels and table headers are small sentence-case text in Graphite 3. There are no uppercase tracked labels above headings.

## Layout

Content sits on a 72rem column with a fluid gutter (16px to 40px). The first screen sets the pitch on a 12-column grid: the h1 in the left eight columns, the lede bottom-aligned in the right four. Below it, the path band spans the full content width with four columns weighted 1 : 1 : 2 : 1, a 32px gap (40px at 1600px and wider), so step 3's station gets the room for the download.

Content sections use a two-column head: a 20rem head column and the body, 64px apart. Section titles and the full setup steps' sticky heads live in the head column. Sections are separated by a hairline and padded fluidly (72px to 128px vertically); the first screen, header and footer sit on Paper Subtle, the content sections on Paper.

Spacing is a loose 4px rhythm: 4 and 8 for tight inline gaps, 12 to 16 between related lines, 20 to 24 inside panels, 40 to 72 between blocks.

**Responsive behaviour.**
- **1100px and up:** the path is horizontal, with the bracket ("You only need step 3") spanning steps 1 and 2 above the rail, and the non-Windows note in two balanced lines just under the station.
- **Below 1100px:** the pitch stacks, with the lede at the measure; the path turns vertical, one rail down the left of the nodes, capped at 40rem; the station indents to the step text and its node sits on the rail; the bracket becomes a plain line above the steps.
- **Below 1024px:** section heads stack; the setup steps drop their sticky heads and the node sits in a 56px left margin; measurements go to two columns.
- **Below 900px:** the signal path stacks vertically with downward arrows; the limits list goes to one column.
- **Below 720px:** the section links leave the header row for a row of their own under it, 44px tall and flush with the gutter (13px with tighter padding below 360px, so all four always fit); facts and measurements go to one column; file rows stack with the download button under the file.
- **Below 640px:** the header's top row drops to 56px and the lockup to 32px; the first screen's top padding drops to 28px and path step descriptions hide, so the download stays inside the first screen; the ready state's links become a ruled list of 44px rows; the setup rail hides and steps are divided by hairlines, and a ruled list that ends a step or a section drops its closing rule so it never sits just above a divider.
- **Below 480px:** the download button's size moves to its own line.
- **Below 360px:** each copy button drops under its hash or command, and "Build from source" may wrap. Nothing scrolls sideways at 320px (400% zoom on a 1280px window).

**The Text Size Rule.** Breakpoints are set in em, so the widths above are at the browser's default 16px text. A visitor who raises the browser's text size gets the layout of a narrower window, never squeezed columns: at 200% text a 1440px window takes the 720px layout. Controls that hold text grow with it (min-height, never a fixed height), and the path's nodes are `max(32px, 2rem)`.

**The Download in the First Screen Rule.** At every width, step 3's primary action is visible without scrolling. When space runs short, cut the descriptions of the other steps, never the station. At 390x844 the download button ends at 679px and every other state's primary action by 806px. On short desktop windows (1100px wide or more and 680px tall or less, such as a 1080p laptop at 150% scaling, about 1280x610) the first screen's top padding and the path's top margin drop to 40px, so every state's primary action ends by 544px.

**The Thumb Rule.** On a touch screen (`pointer: coarse`) every target is at least 44px tall: buttons, copy buttons, nav links, the SHA-256 summary, footer links and every link that stands on its own line. Those grow in the flow, with negative margins that keep their text where it was. A link inside a sentence keeps its line and gets a 44px invisible hit area instead. Mouse layouts keep their density.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat. Depth comes from tone (Paper Subtle bands, Surface panels, Sunken wells) and 1px hairlines, not from shadow. One shadow exists: a tight lift of a single pixel, used to separate the containers that hold the download and the file list from the page.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Lift** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgb(23 24 27 / 0.07)`; dark: `0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.5)`): the station panel, the full download notice, the file list, and the knobs stage in the signal path. Nothing else.

### Named Rules
**The One Lift Rule.** A shadow is never larger than the lift. If a container needs more presence, give it a stronger hairline or the ink border, not a deeper shadow.

## Shapes

Gently rounded rectangles on hairline borders. Containers that hold content (the station, file lists, disclosures, notes, signal stages) use the 12px radius; buttons, code blocks and the hash panel use 8px; the copy button and nav links 6px; small inline things (the focus ring, kbd keys, the hash summary) 4px. Step nodes are true circles; tags are pills. The external, download, copy, check, chevron, arrow, info and GitHub icons are 16px stroke glyphs (1.5px stroke, round caps), drawn inline from one sprite at 16px, or 12px beside link text.

**The Hairline Structure Rule.** Structure is drawn with 1px lines: rails, row dividers, table rules, section rules, the bracket. A line never grows past 1px except the key cap's 2px bottom edge. The voice trace is the one 1px line that isn't structure.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Shape:** gently rounded (8px), 40px tall, 16px side padding, Plex Sans 500 at 15px, an optional 16px icon 8px from the label.
- **Leading icon:** says where the button goes: the GitHub mark for a page on GitHub ("Watch for releases", "Releases on GitHub"), the down arrow for a panel on this page ("Build from source"), the download icon for a file.
- **Primary:** ink fill, on-ink text. Hover lightens to Ink Lifted.
- **Secondary:** surface fill, strong hairline border, ink text. Hover darkens the border to Graphite 3 and fills Paper Subtle.
- **Download (signature):** a full-width primary button, 60px tall, with a 20px download icon and two lines: "Download knobs" (600, 16px) and a sub-line at the label size, 78% opacity, with tabular figures ("Installer for Windows x64 · 4.5 MB").
- **Build from source:** a secondary button with the 16px down arrow and two lines: "Build from source", then "(Visual Studio 2022 and CMake)" at the label size in Graphite 2, so the cost is read before the click. Every one of them jumps to the "Build it from source" panel in the full step 3 (`#build`), which opens on arrival.
- **Active:** every button presses down 1px. Transitions run 160ms on the standard curve.
- **Focus:** a 2px ink outline, 2px offset, 4px radius, everywhere on the page.

### Links
- Underlined at 1px with a 0.22em offset; the underline sits in Hairline Strong at rest and takes the text colour on hover.
- Links that leave the site carry the 12px external arrow after the text; in-page jumps use the down arrow. A visitor can tell from the icon where a link goes.
- The header "Source" link is a small outlined button with the GitHub mark, 34px tall.

### Step Rail and Nodes (signature)
- **Path (first screen):** four round nodes (32px, Plex Sans 600 numerals) on a 1px Hairline Strong rail. Idle nodes are hollow with Graphite 2 numerals. Step 3's node is inked.
- **Setup (full steps):** 40px nodes, sticky at 32px from the top beside each step's head on desktop, on a vertical rail. As a step reaches the reading line (45% of the viewport height), its node fills with ink and the rail segment above the next step draws in from the top (320ms).
- **Bracket:** a 1px open bracket over steps 1 and 2, 12px above the nodes, whose ticks run down to the path. The left one starts at node 1's centre, so the node covers its end and it lands on the node's top; the right one meets the rail in the middle of the gap before step 3. Its label-size line, 8px above it, tells experienced users they only need step 3 and stays between the ticks: from 1100px to about 1170px wide it breaks after the question.
- **Forced colours:** the rails are drawn as borders, so they survive, and inked nodes fill with the visitor's own text colour (`CanvasText`, numeral in `Canvas`).

### Station (signature)
- The one bordered panel on the first screen: Surface fill, Hairline Strong border, 12px radius, the lift, 24px padding. Its node breaks the top border.
- It lays out every release state in one grid cell and shows only the active one, so the panel keeps the height of the tallest state (today the none state) and never changes size between states. Without JavaScript only the static state takes room.
- **Static / no JS:** "From GitHub Releases", with "Releases on GitHub" (primary) and "Build from source" (secondary). Without JavaScript the line reads "Releases, their files and checksums are on GitHub.", which claims no release.
- **Loading:** the static text reads "Checking GitHub for the latest release…", at the same panel height as every answer.
- **Ready:** the download button, the github.com destination in mono under it, then a label-size line for visitors who skip steps 1 and 2: "Needs OBS 32.2.x"; then the date with a "Pre-release" tag when it applies, release notes, then a links row (portable zip with size and the download icon, checksums, all releases). "Checksums" opens every file's SHA-256 in the full step 3 as it lands there. Below 640px the date and release notes become the first row of a ruled list, and the three links its other rows, label left and icon right.
- **None:** "No release yet", "Watch for releases" (primary) and "Build from source" (secondary), then a label-size hint: "With a GitHub account: Watch › Custom › Releases. Without one, add the releases feed to a feed reader.", the feed linking to `releases.atom`.
- **Error:** "Couldn't check for a release", the reason, "Releases on GitHub" (primary) and "Try again" (secondary). When GitHub's rate limit was hit, "Try again" is left out, since asking again can't work until the limit resets, and the page keeps that answer until then (between 30 seconds and 15 minutes) so a reload doesn’t ask again.

- **Not on Windows:** each state carries a quiet label-size note (Graphite 3, balanced lines, "Windows 64-bit PC." never split), before its first action and never moving it. In ready it sits directly under the download button, above the github.com line: "This is the installer for a Windows 64-bit PC. knobs doesn’t run on phones or macOS." ("the portable zip" or "Its files are" when the button gets something else). None: "Both will be for a Windows 64-bit PC." Error: "The files there are for a Windows 64-bit PC." Loading: "knobs is for a Windows 64-bit PC. It doesn’t run on phones or macOS." On the horizontal path (1100px and up) the note sits just under the panel instead.

### Voice Trace (decoration)
- One spoken phrase, drawn as a single 1px line in Hairline Strong through the peak and trough of each 4px slice of the recording, 14px from the zero line at the loudest peak. It is the recording's own outline, generated from the WAV, never drawn by hand.
- Its zero line is the seam under the first screen, centred on the next section's rule, so the trace reads as that rule picking up a voice. Quiet stretches aren't drawn, and the rule shows through them. It starts at the content's left edge.
- It lives in the band's bottom padding and the top of the next section, never behind the headline or the download. At its tightest (1100px wide, with the non-Windows note under the panel) it clears the nearest text by about 14px. It is positioned out of the flow, so it never moves the download.
- One unit is one pixel and it is never squeezed. A narrower column shows the start of the phrase, and a stretch of sound that would cross the column's edge isn't drawn (each path carries its right edge as `--end`, and its stroke width drops to 0 when the column is narrower), so the line always stops in a pause, never mid-word. Phones show the first three words.
- No fill, gradient, glow or motion; the setup rail stays the only thing that moves. Inline SVG, `aria-hidden`, its stroke the Hairline Strong token so dark mode follows; hidden in forced colours.
- **Placeholder recording.** The current trace is macOS `say` reading the headline. The voice recording from knobs's bit-identical tests replaces it; that recording needs real pauses (quiet below the floor, 0.6px at this scale), or the phrase draws as one stretch and narrow columns show none of it.

**The One Decoration Rule.** The voice trace is the page's only decoration: a real recording's outline at 1px in Hairline Strong, static, at the seam under the first screen. Never a drawn, repeating or animated wave, never a second decoration, and never anything behind the headline or the download.

### File Rows
- A Surface list with a strong hairline border, 12px radius and the lift; rows separated by hairlines, 18px by 22px padding.
- Each row: the file kind in 600, then the file name (Graphite 2) and size (Graphite 3) in Plex Mono; a secondary "Download" button on the right.
- **SHA-256, collapsed:** under each file, a small "SHA-256" summary with a chevron that turns 90 degrees on open. It opens to a Sunken panel (8px radius) with the full hash in Plex Mono in even rows of 32, a copy button, and a one-line hint: "Run this in PowerShell. It prints True if the file matches." The command under it, `(Get-FileHash "$HOME\Downloads\<file>" -Algorithm SHA256).Hash -eq '<sha>'`, prints True or False and ignores case. A wrap never splits an option from its dash, and the hash runs on from `-eq` as a terminal would wrap it. A file with no SHA-256 on GitHub gets the command without the comparison and a hint to compare by hand. The station's "Checksums" link, or arriving at `#files`, opens every row's SHA-256 at once.
- The main file (the installer, or the portable zip when there's no installer) gets the ink "Download" button. It also gets a "Recommended" tag after its kind, but only when the release has another copy of knobs to choose from; a lone file goes untagged.
- A label-size line under the list names its source on github.com.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** put the download inside the bordered station as step 3 of the path, with the destination on github.com shown under the button.
- **Do** use ink for every primary action and the surface-plus-hairline style for every secondary one.
- **Do** set anything copyable or comparable (versions, file names, file sizes, hashes, commands) in Plex Mono, and line numbers up with tabular figures.
- **Do** cap text with the `--measure` token (52ch), never in rem.
- **Do** mark every link that leaves the site with the 12px external arrow.
- **Do** keep every release state the same size, and let state changes settle with opacity and a 4px rise on `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`: 160ms for feedback, 320ms for structure. Reduced motion makes every change instant: no animation or transition at all, never a 0.01ms duration, which would hold a newly shown state hidden (and unable to take focus) until the next frame.
- **Do** give every new colour role both a light and a `-dark` value.
- **Do** say plainly that knobs isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the OBS Project, in the footer of any page.
- **Do** keep the lockup at 32px tall or more, swapped by colour scheme, with clear space at least the pointer's length.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use brand red outside the logo files.
- **Don't** add gradients, glass or blur to the page. The knob mark's own shading belongs to the logo and is not a pattern to reuse.
- **Don't** use any shadow larger than the one-pixel lift, and don't use hard offset shadows.
- **Don't** put icons in coloured tiles or circles; icons are bare 16px strokes beside text.
- **Don't** add imagery the project doesn’t have: no invented screenshots, illustrations or stock art.
- **Don't** add decoration beyond the voice trace: no background waves, patterns or textures, and no second trace.
- **Don't** load anything from a third party. Fonts, icons and logos are served from the site; the GitHub releases API is the only outside request.
- **Don't** set any page text in Sora or ship its font file; Sora lives only in the outlined logo. Don't add uppercase tracked labels above headings.
- **Don't** animate content in on scroll; only the setup rail responds to scrolling.
- **Don't** show the name as anything but lowercase "knobs", even at the start of a sentence or in a heading.
